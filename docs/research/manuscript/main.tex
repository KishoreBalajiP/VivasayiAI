\title{Evidence-Gated Crop-Loss Claim Verification and Profile-Aware Advisory Chat for Smallholder Farmers in Tamil Nadu: System Design and Synthetic Evaluation}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Author names withheld in this draft}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Vivasayi AI project}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Smallholder crop-loss claims are hard to verify: a farmer's photographs, a land document, a polygon drawn on a map and a satellite vegetation index each say something different, and none alone establishes ownership or loss. We describe Vivasayi AI, a backend that combines a farmer-aware advisory chat with a claim-verification pipeline in which every evidence source is reduced to an explicit per-component outcome (verified, failed, unverified, unavailable, inconclusive, pending, requires review) and a deterministic rule engine, never a language model, decides. A required-component gate makes an approval depend on claimant-rights evidence, and a provider failure is never read as a negative finding. We evaluate the implementation on deterministic synthetic benchmarks with independently derived or specification-based ground truth. The geometry checks agree with analytic ground truth, the legacy photograph-only policy approved 10 of 190 scenarios that the design rule forbids while the gate approved none, and the context logic asked no unnecessary follow-up questions where the previous rule asked in most cases. We also report a systematic 0.6\% overestimate of area in the server's formula. \emph{These results show that the components enforce their rules; they are not measurements on real claims, documents or imagery, none of which were available.} Live satellite and language-model validation and all real-data experiments remain pending, and we state what each requires.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Index and indemnity crop insurance depend on knowing whether a loss occurred, on which land, and who may claim it. Remote sensing is widely used to design area indices and to monitor parcels \cite{okeyo2020,sitokonstantinou2018}, but basis risk remains a recognised problem and resolution and timing affect it \cite{kolle2021,dalhaus2016,carter2017}. In India, trust is a barrier to take-up \cite{cole2013}. At the same time, large language models and vision-language models can read documents and photographs \cite{gemini2023,kim2022}, but hallucination is a documented risk \cite{ji2023} and, for high-stakes decisions, interpretable rules are preferred to opaque models \cite{rudin2019}.

This paper describes a system built on that premise: models produce \emph{evidence}; a deterministic engine produces the \emph{decision}; and every decision is tied to a versioned policy and a snapshot of the evidence used. We make three contributions that we can support, and are explicit about those we cannot:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A design in which each evidence source maps to an explicit per-component outcome, with provider failure and absence of evidence kept distinct from negative findings, and a required-component gate that can only make an approval more cautious.
\item An open implementation of the pipeline, including context-sufficiency logic for a bilingual advisory chat, parcel-geometry validation, cross-farmer overlap as a review signal, document comparison with Tamil/Latin name matching, and a pre/post-event NDVI evidence step.
\item A reproducible synthetic evaluation, with its limitations stated, and a protocol for the real-data experiments that remain to be done.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim novelty of the individual techniques, which have precedent, nor any accuracy on real claims. Section~\ref{sec:gap} states what a literature search found and did not find.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}\label{sec:gap}
\textbf{Satellite damage assessment.} Sentinel-2 \cite{drusch2012} and NDVI \cite{tucker1979} are standard for vegetation monitoring, and pre/post-event differencing has been used to map crop damage \cite{ursu2025}. Cloud and shadow masking of Sentinel-2 needs validation and benchmark data exist \cite{baetens2019,raiyani2021,aybar2022}. Satellite yield estimation can track ground measures on smallholder plots \cite{lobell2020,burke2017}.

\textbf{Advisory assistants.} Retrieval-augmented generation \cite{lewis2020,gao2023} has been applied to agriculture with domain constraints \cite{ravindran2026}, and personalised farmer chatbots have been deployed \cite{singh2024}. Voice advisory in India has been evaluated \cite{cole2021}. Vision-language models are weak on fine-grained agricultural recognition \cite{shinoda2025}.

\textbf{Documents.} General document-understanding methods exist \cite{kim2022,xu2020}.

\textbf{Gap.} In our search (abstracts and metadata of 45 verified sources, searched 16 October 2026) we did not find work on verifying crop-loss claims by fusing photograph, document, satellite and weather components under a required-component gate; on cross-farmer polygon overlap as a signal in crop insurance; on Tamil-language agricultural retrieval or Tamil/Latin land-document name matching; or a public labelled dataset of Indian claim outcomes. Absence in a narrow search is weak evidence; we therefore make no priority claim, and describe the combination as a design whose effectiveness is untested.

\section{System Design}
The backend is a Node.js service on AWS Lambda with MongoDB. A farmer registers parcels as polygons; a claim is made against a registered parcel with a drawn affected area. The server computes area, validates geometry, and never accepts area, state or decision from the client.

\subsection{Evidence components and outcomes}
Ten components describe a claim: parcel identity, claimant rights, land documents, geometry, crop, satellite, photographs, weather, overlap and manual review. Each resolves to one of seven outcomes. A provider that errors or is disabled yields \emph{unavailable}; missing evidence yields \emph{unverified}; \emph{failed} requires a positive finding that contradicts the claim. Evidence derived by a model (photographs, document reading) is never authoritative, and a model reading alone can never verify claimant rights. Satellite and weather are supporting evidence and are never \emph{failed}.

\subsection{Deterministic decision and the gate}
A frozen rule engine applies, in order: claim window, event type, area allowance, containment in the parcel, duplicate or partial overlap with the farmer's own claims, remaining eligible area, and the photograph assessment. A versioned policy then names which components are required. An approval whose required component is unverified or failed becomes a request for more evidence; one whose required component is pending, unavailable, inconclusive or needs review is \emph{held}, remaining submitted and withdrawable. The gate never produces a rejection. Under the stricter policy a confident ``no damage'' model reading holds the claim for a person instead of rejecting it. Each decision stores the policy identifier and a hash of its content, the component outcomes, and references to the evidence rows used.

\subsection{Geometry and overlap}
Polygons are validated (closed ring, finite in-range coordinates, no self-intersection, non-zero area). Containment uses a tolerance. Overlap with another farmer's claim on the same land is detected through a bounding-box pre-filter and an exact intersection, and is recorded as a review signal only: shared boundaries, joint holdings and cadastral error are legitimate causes of overlap.

\subsection{Documents and claimant relationship}
A vision-language model reads a land document into structured fields; the document is treated as untrusted data and instruction-like text inside it is itself a finding. Extracted holder names are compared with the claimant across Tamil and Latin scripts by a consonant-skeleton matcher. The claimant declares a relationship to the land (owner, joint owner, tenant, authorised cultivator, heir); each has a different expected document set, and a name differing from the recorded holder is expected, not a discrepancy, for all but owners. This policy is provisional and unreviewed by any legal or insurer authority.

\subsection{Satellite evidence}
For each claim the system requests a pre-event and a post-event mean NDVI over the claim polygon from the Copernicus Data Space Statistical API \cite{cdse_statistical}, excluding pixels the scene classification marks as cloud, shadow, cirrus, saturated or no-data. Water is deliberately not masked so that flooding stays observable, which is also a confound for paddy. The change is interpreted as supporting loss, no change, or inconclusive (too few valid pixels), by provisional thresholds. A change in a vegetation index does not identify a cause. Historical rainfall \cite{openmeteo_hist} is recorded as supporting evidence only. \emph{This path has not been run against the live service.}

\subsection{Profile-aware advisory chat}
For each message the assistant classifies intent, loads only the context that intent needs from the farmer's own records, tags each item with its provenance (user-provided, verified, third-party, inferred, missing, stale), and asks a follow-up only when a required slot (crop, district) cannot be resolved, and then about exactly one thing. Retrieved knowledge is fenced and labelled as data. Replies follow the requested or detected language.

\section{Experimental Setup}
All evaluations are deterministic, seeded and reproducible from the repository (\texttt{npm run eval}); the environment and commit are recorded with each result. Ground truth is one of three kinds, always stated: analytic (geometry), the design rule (fusion), or a written specification (chat); the name set is author-labelled. No credentials, network or real data were used.

\section{Discussion and Error Analysis}
The chief finding is structural: separating evidence from decision, and failure from negative finding, makes behaviour under provider outages and missing data explicit and testable. A secondary finding is that an evaluation harness found real defects (range validation, systematic area bias, glide letters in name matching). Errors in the synthetic benchmarks are few precisely because ground truth and implementation share their assumptions; the informative cases are the ones the harness corrected.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item No real claims, documents or imagery were used. No accuracy, fraud-detection or loss-estimation figure is claimed.
\item Specifications and labels are author-defined; the name matcher was tuned on its own evaluation set.
\item Live language-model and satellite behaviour is unvalidated. NDVI thresholds are provisional, and paddy flooding and normal senescence can change NDVI without damage.
\item No authorised Tamil Nadu land-record API is available; the official-record adapter is provider-neutral and returns \emph{unavailable}.
\item The required-document policy per claimant relationship is unreviewed.
\item Cloud-mask reliability for small parcels was not evaluated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethical, Legal and Privacy Considerations}
No real farmer data were used. Real use needs informed consent, secure handling of documents, permission for any official record, and human review of adverse and uncertain outcomes; by design no model reading alone rejects a claim under the strict policy. The statement is in the repository (\texttt{docs/research/ethics\_privacy\_legal.md}); it has not been reviewed by a lawyer or ethics board.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
We described and tested an evidence-gated claim-verification design and a profile-aware chat. The results show the components enforce their specifications; they do not show real-world accuracy. The next steps are the pending experiments: live satellite validation against independently labelled events, rated assistant answers, labelled document extraction, and evaluation on authorised cadastral data.
